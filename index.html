How your phone reaches your computer

Pick a situation to see the path. Whichever way it goes, the traffic is encrypted and your computer only answers devices you paired.

Same Wi-Fi: direct

Scan the pairing code your computer shows, once. From then on the phone finds the computer on your home network by itself and talks to it over HTTPS pinned to that one computer's certificate — nothing else on the Wi-Fi can pretend to be it.

Away from home: Tailscale

Install the free Tailscale app on both devices and sign in with the same account. The phone reaches the computer through an encrypted private network; if Tailscale is off on the phone, the app says so in plain words.

Coming: the relay

A small relay will carry messages between your paired devices when they cannot see each other — end-to-end encrypted, so the relay only ever holds sealed envelopes it cannot open. No extra app to install.

The guide, step by step

Open only the step you need. The app itself has a guided setup and a help assistant that answers from this same guide.

1Install and open
  1. Download the app (see “Get the app” below) and allow installing it when Android asks.
  2. The first screens ask how you want answers, which languages you speak and read, and a short note about you — all stay on the phone.
2Choose your brain
  1. Settings → AI models: paste a key from any provider you use. Each card links to the page where that provider issues keys.
  2. No key? Install Ollama on your computer and pull a model — the app finds it and uses it for free.
  3. “Key check” shows each key's balance and warns before one runs out.
3Connect your computer (preview)
  1. Start the Braining bridge on the computer. It is set up by hand for now; an installer is on the way — follow the channel.
  2. On the computer, open the pairing page and point the phone's camera at the code. Tap the link, then “Pair”.
  3. At home it connects over your Wi-Fi; away, over Tailscale. The pairing page lists every paired device and removes any of them.
4Talk and listen
  1. Tap the microphone and speak; the words appear as you talk. Edit them, then send.
  2. “Improve” rewords what you wrote, in the same language, and “Undo” brings it back.
  3. Settings → Voice: choose who reads answers aloud — the phone, a cloud voice on your key, or your computer.
5Pictures and files
  1. Ask for a picture in plain words. The fast free model draws first; tap another model under it for a higher-quality version.
  2. While it draws you can switch to another model; if one fails, the next free one takes over and the app says why.
  3. Attach a photo and ask about it, attach a text file, or an audio file to turn into text.
6Braining mode
  1. Turn it on for a bigger task. The app asks focused questions one at a time; skip any you like.
  2. It writes a plan, gives each step to the model best suited to it, and shows the result of every step.
?When something does not work
  • “Tailscale is off on this phone”: open Tailscale, tap Connect, and set it as an always-on VPN in Android's settings.
  • “The computer did not answer”: check the computer is awake and the bridge is running, and that both are on the same Wi-Fi at home.
  • A key refused: “Key check” in Settings says which one and why.

Privacy, in one screen

Not a promise but a consequence of how it is built.

  • Nothing is collected. No account, no analytics, no advertising id; the app never asks for your location.
  • Your keys are encrypted in the phone's secure hardware and never copied to a cloud backup.
  • Your words go only where you send them — to the model you chose, or to your own computer.
  • Raw audio is never kept — it becomes text and is deleted, on the phone and on the computer.
  • No diagnostic report leaves unless you tap send, after seeing it.

Questions people ask

Is it free?

The app is free. Models on your own computer cost nothing; cloud models cost whatever their provider charges your key — the app shows the price class before you pick one.

Do I need a powerful computer?

No computer is needed at all if you use cloud keys. For the free features, a graphics card helps: on a laptop card with 6 GB, a quick picture takes seconds, a high-quality one a few minutes, and dictation is near-instant.

Isn't it just a wrapper?

It is a client, and says so. What it adds: voice that works in Arabic dialects, questions before answers, free fallbacks when a model fails, and a way to put your own computer to work.

Can the app control my computer without me?

No. The bridge only answers devices you paired, asks before risky actions, refuses credential folders outright, and keeps a log on your computer that the app cannot edit. There is no remote access for anyone else — building one was refused.
